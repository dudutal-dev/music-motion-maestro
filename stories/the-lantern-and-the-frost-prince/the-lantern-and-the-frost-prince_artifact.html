The Lantern and the Frost Princea story of narnia

Deep in the snowy wood there stood one iron lamp-post. Nell lit it every single night. She was the last lamplighter left in all of Narnia.

The winter had lasted a hundred years. No one walked the snowy path anymore. So Nell talked to the little flame, so that neither of them would be lonely.

One night the light fell on something new. A statue of clear ice. A young man, frozen in the middle of a step, one hand reaching out.

Nell brushed the snow from his face. "Good evening," she said. She said it every night for a week. On the seventh night, the ice blinked.

His name was Aren. He could only wake while the lamp was burning. Warm light gave him breath. When the flame went out at dawn, he turned back to ice. So Nell fed the flame all night long. And Aren told her about the world before the winter.

They walked out onto the frozen river together. He showed her the drowned bells sleeping under the ice. She showed him a patch of green moss beneath a stone, the last green thing in the wood.

Night after night they laughed, and the long winter nights began to feel far too short. Nell burned her chair. She burned her books. She burned her wooden shoes, just to keep the flame alive.

Then the oil ran out. White frost came creeping up the lamp-post. "Let me freeze," said Aren. "Go home and be warm."

But Nell did not go home. She struck her last spark into the dry moss. She held his freezing hand in both of hers, and she would not let go, all through the night, all the way to the dawn.

Far away over the eastern hills, a lion roared. The sound rolled through the trees like thunder made of gold. And the ice began to crack.

Spring came back to Narnia in a single morning. Aren woke up warm, with his heart beating, holding her hands.

Snowdrops opened everywhere. And still, every evening, the two of them lit the old lamp together, for anyone else who might be lost in the wood.
